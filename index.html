Question 01

AssetAlign matches your employees with vetted financial advisors and delivers a real, personalized financial plan to every single one of them — at zero cost to you, and zero cost to them. Every employee. Regardless of income, account size, or where they're starting from.

Where most "financial wellness" platforms hand your team another app to log into, we hand them a person to call. That's the whole product.

Question 03
Are AssetAlign and the advisors fiduciaries to our employees?

Yes — and we built it this way on purpose. ABTW Corporation, d/b/a AssetAlign, is registered as an investment adviser with the Massachusetts Securities Division. Every advisor in our network is independently licensed and individually fiduciary to the employee they're matched with. Their job is to act in your employee's best interest. Full stop.

Advisor compensation is structured so there are no kickbacks for steering employees into specific products. Compare that to the version of "free financial wellness" most companies have already seen — where the "advisor" turns out to be a product-pushing rep paid on commission. That was never going to be us.

Question 04
Does offering AssetAlign create any fiduciary liability for us?

No — and that was a design requirement, not an accident. ABTW Corporation, d/b/a AssetAlign, is the state-registered investment adviser of record, registered with the Massachusetts Securities Division and not with the SEC. The independent advisors in our network owe their fiduciary duty directly to the employee they're working with — not to you.

Because the benefit is voluntary, employee-elected, not funded by you, and not endorsed by you in the ERISA sense, it sits inside the Department of Labor's voluntary plan safe harbor at 29 C.F.R. § 2510.3-1(j). That's the same structure the rest of the workplace financial wellness category relies on to keep employers out of co-fiduciary territory.

Run it past your own ERISA counsel before rollout, of course — and we're happy to share the full legal framework on a follow-up call.

Question 06
How are the advisors on AssetAlign actually regulated?

Financial advisors are some of the most-regulated professionals in the country. Four layers of accountability stack up behind every conversation an employee has.

Individual level. Every advisor holds the required securities licenses (Series 65 or 66 for fiduciary investment advice; Series 7, 6, or 63 for brokerage work where applicable) plus any state insurance licenses. Their full career record — every employer, disclosure, complaint, and regulatory action — is publicly searchable on FINRA BrokerCheck and the SEC's IAPD.

Firm level. Each advisor operates under a Registered Investment Adviser or broker-dealer that files an annual Form ADV, employs a Chief Compliance Officer, maintains a written compliance program under SEC Rule 206(4)-7, and is subject to periodic regulator examination.

Trade level. Inside each firm, recommendations are reviewed by compliance for suitability, best execution, and conflicts of interest. Regulation Best Interest applies to brokerage activity; full fiduciary duty applies to advisory work. Any disclosure event updates the public BrokerCheck record within days.

AssetAlign level. We rescreen every advisor annually against BrokerCheck, IAPD, and state regulator filings, and require network advisors to sign a written code of conduct that goes beyond the regulatory minimum — no product-pushing, no kickbacks, no misaligned compensation.

Question 08
What if some of our employees already have a financial advisor?

That's the most common pattern — and it's not a problem. AssetAlign complements; it doesn't displace. Employees with an existing advisor keep them. AssetAlign is still useful as a second opinion, for their spouse or adult children, or for situations their primary advisor doesn't focus on.

The bigger reason this matters: most of your workforce probably doesn't have an advisor — usually because they assume they don't have "enough money to be worth it" to one. AssetAlign removes that barrier.

Question 09
How do we measure engagement and prove the impact?

HR teams get periodic engagement summaries — aggregated and anonymized — showing how much of your workforce is actively engaging, how often, and the kinds of financial situations being worked on.

Individual employee conversations are never shared with the employer. What you get is exactly what you need to communicate the value internally to leadership — without compromising the trust your employees place in their advisor relationship.
